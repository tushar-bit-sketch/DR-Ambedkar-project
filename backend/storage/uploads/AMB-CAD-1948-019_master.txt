CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY OF INDIA
Thursday, the 9th December 1948

Draft Constitution — Debate on Draft Article 25 (Article 32 of the Constitution of India)

The Honourable Dr. B. R. Ambedkar:

"Sir, I am very glad that the majority of those who spoke on this article have recognized its paramount importance. If I was asked to name any particular article in this Constitution as the most important—an article without which this Constitution would be a nullity—I could not refer to any other article except this one. It is the very soul of the Constitution and the very heart of it and I am glad that the House has realized its importance.

Hereafter it would not be open to any person to say that the Fundamental Rights are mere pious declarations, because under Article 25 (Article 32), an ordinary citizen is given the right to approach the Supreme Court directly by appropriate proceedings for the enforcement of the rights conferred by Part III of the Constitution.

The Supreme Court is constituted as the protector and guarantor of Fundamental Rights, and the power to issue directions, orders, or writs, including writs in the nature of habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, quo warranto and certiorari, whichever may be appropriate, is explicitly conferred upon the Court. 

This provision ensures that constitutional remedies are themselves a fundamental right, thereby establishing constitutional morality as the bedrock of Indian democracy."

[Source: Constituent Assembly Debates (Official Report), Vol. VII, Lok Sabha Secretariat, New Delhi, pp. 950-953]
